Phoenix Pool Resurfacing: Finishes, Costs and Timing

Updated 2026 • Phoenix, AZ • Pool resurfacing

Phoenix is hard on pool surfaces. With more than 160 days a year above 90°F, strong sun and hard water, interior finishes wear faster here than in most of the country. This guide covers the signs that your pool needs resurfacing, how local conditions affect each finish, what the work typically costs across the Phoenix metro, and how to plan the project.

Signs your pool needs resurfacing

A surface that feels rough, stains that return soon after brushing, white scale lines at the waterline, chips, hollow-sounding patches and faded or blotchy color are the most common signs. A porous surface also makes the water harder to balance, so rising chemical use is another clue.

Small surface cracks are usually repaired during the job. Cracks that keep growing, or signs that the shell has moved, should be checked first, because a new finish needs a stable shell.

How Phoenix heat and water affect your surface

Most of the Valley's water comes through the Central Arizona Project and local sources that are high in calcium and minerals. Combined with high water temperatures in summer and fast evaporation, that leads to scale, staining and etching. Intense UV also fades color and breaks down the surface over time.

That is why harder, more stain-resistant finishes such as quartz and pebble tend to give better long-term value in Phoenix, and why steady water chemistry matters as much as the finish itself.

Choosing a finish

Plaster is the lowest upfront cost and gives a bright, classic look, but it has the shortest life in Arizona conditions.

Quartz adds colored quartz to the mix for a harder, more stain-resistant surface. It is a solid mid-range choice.

Pebble finishes such as Pebble Tec are the most durable option in heat and hard water. They cost more upfront but often come out cheaper per year over the life of the surface.

What pool resurfacing costs in Phoenix

For a typical residential pool with about 400 to 500 square feet of interior surface, common ranges are:

FinishTypical costNotes
Plaster$4,000 to $5,500Lowest upfront cost, shortest life in Arizona
Quartz$6,000 to $8,000Stain resistant, good mid-range value
Pebble Tec$7,500 to $12,000+Most durable, best long-term value

Most standard projects in the Phoenix metro land between about $4,500 and $9,200. Crack repair, new waterline tile, coping and equipment work are usually quoted separately, and larger pools or attached spas add surface area and cost.

How the project works

  • Inspection and quote: the contractor measures the surface, checks cracks and hollow spots, and reviews finish options with you.
  • Drain and prep: the pool is drained and the old surface is prepared so the new finish bonds, with cracks repaired.
  • Tile and fittings: waterline tile, coping and lights are handled if they are part of the job.
  • Application: the new finish is applied in one day for an even cure.
  • Fill and start-up: the pool is refilled immediately, then the water is balanced and the surface brushed daily during the first weeks.

October through March is the preferred window in Phoenix. Cooler weather helps the finish cure evenly, and the pool is ready before the long swim season.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does pool resurfacing take in Phoenix?

Most projects take about one to two weeks from draining to swimming, depending on repairs and tile work.

Why does resurfacing cost more in Phoenix than the national average?

Heat, UV and hard water push many owners toward more durable finishes, and those finishes and the prep they need cost more. Local labor and demand also play a part.

Which finish lasts longest in Arizona?

Pebble finishes usually last longest in Phoenix heat and hard water, followed by quartz. Plain plaster wears fastest.

When is the best time to resurface a pool in Phoenix?

October through March. Milder temperatures help the new surface cure evenly and get the pool ready before summer.
